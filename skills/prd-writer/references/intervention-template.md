# Intervention PRD template

For programmes, processes, behaviour change, or org-level initiatives. Structured around problem, theory of change, intervention, and rollout.

British English. Em dashes only where they earn their place (default to parentheses, commas, colons, or sentence breaks). Standard markdown only: H1/H2/H3, bullets, numbered lists, tables, `---` dividers, `>` callouts, fenced code blocks. No Notion-specific syntax.

---

```markdown
# [Project name]

**Owner:** [name]   **Status:** Draft   **Last updated:** [date]

---

## TL;DR
3 to 4 sentences. What this is, who it is for, why now, what ships in v1.

## 1. What is the problem we are trying to solve?

### Qualitative problem (what it feels like)
~150 words. Day-to-day feel of the problem.

### What this looks like in practice
Anecdotal signals as a bulleted list of concrete patterns.

### Impact on OGP
Bulleted consequences: turnaround, experience, risk, scalability, accountability.

### Quantitative problem (what the data tells us)
Bulleted data points, or gaps where baseline needs to be established.

> For small asks: collapse to Qualitative + Quantitative only. Do not cut rigour, just structure.

## 2. Intervention & Theory of Change

### Intervention
Bulleted. What we are actually doing.

### Theory of change
Three lines, do not collapse:
1. **What we will do** (the intervention).
2. **What we expect to happen** (the mechanism: how the intervention produces the outcome). This is the load-bearing claim.
3. **Why we believe this will work** (evidence or reasoning supporting the mechanism). If this reads hollow, the theory is thin.

## 3. Alternatives considered

| Option | Why considered | Why ruled out |
|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |

At least two options. Include "do nothing" if relevant.

## 4. Users
Short list or table. Not prose.

## 5. Current vs ideal user journey

### Current journey
Bulleted. Where work originates, how it gets handled, where it fails.

### Ideal journey (post-intervention)
Bulleted mirror.

### User stories
"As a [role], I want [capability] so that [outcome]." One per affected user type.

## 6. Goals / Objectives

### Qualitative goals
If this works, what changes. Bulleted.

### Quantitative goals (mandatory table, no exceptions)

| Metric | Baseline | Target | Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |

If no baseline exists, the entry reads "establish baseline within N weeks". That is the commitment.

### How will this be measured?
Short: system of record, reporting cadence, review forum.

## 7. Non-goals & Guardrails

### Non-goals
Bulleted. Tempting-but-no features.

### Guardrail metrics
Signals that, if they move the wrong way, trigger pause and reassessment.

## 8. Assumptions
Each framed as: *what we believe* then *what we are betting on* then *what would prove it wrong*. At least three.

### We acknowledge
Bulleted failure modes: conditions under which the intervention does not produce the outcome.

## 9. Risks & Unknowns

### Risks
Identified threats with mitigations.

### Unknowns
Things we do not know yet and need to monitor. Each with an intent to learn.

## 10. Change Management & Operationalisation

*Include only when the PRD involves people or behaviour change. Skip for automation-only.*

### Approach
2 to 3 sentences framing rollout philosophy.

### Phase 1: [name] (weeks X to Y)
**Objective.** One line.
**Key actions.** Bulleted.
**Outputs.** Bulleted.

Repeat for as many phases as the plan needs (typically 2 to 4).

### Change management strategy
Bulleted subsections: Leadership alignment, Behaviour change, Communication plan, Training & enablement, Adoption & accountability. 3 to 5 bullets each.

## 11. Retrospective / After-Action Review

*To be completed after rollout.*

- What did we expect?
- What actually happened?
- What surprised us?
- What needs to change next cycle?
```
